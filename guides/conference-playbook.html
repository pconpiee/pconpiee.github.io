If you read nothing else — the whole game in 3 moves

  1. BEFORE: Pick 5 people you want to meet. Message 3 lapsed contacts who'll be there ("I'll be at X — coffee?"). That's the highest-return hour you'll spend.
  2. DURING: Go to give, not get. Ask questions, listen, make one intro a day. Log every real contact within 5 minutes — your memory is gone by the coffee break.
  3. AFTER: Follow up within 48 hours. 80% of leads get zero follow-up — just doing this beats almost everyone in the room.

0 · Mindset — read this on the train there (60 seconds)

Three ideas that fix the things that actually go wrong at conferences. They're not pep talk — each is a tested finding.

It's going better than it feels

You'll walk away from conversations sure you were awkward. You're wrong, and predictably so — people consistently underestimate how much a stranger liked talking to them. The cringe is a known glitch in self-perception, not feedback. So approach the next person anyway.

Evidence · The "liking gap" — Boothby et al., 2018, Psychological Science. Held even among roommates for months.

Go to give, not to get

Networking feels gross when you show up to extract. The fix isn't to push through it — it's to flip it: come to give an intro, a useful fact, a genuine question. The ick disappears and reciprocity does the rest. People help those who helped them.

Evidence · Instrumental networking can feel "dirty" → people do less of it — Casciaro et al., 2014, Admin. Science Quarterly.
Your one job on the floor Be the person the room is better for having held — curious, useful, easy to talk to. Do that and the referrals, intros, and job leadscustomers, intros, and investor interest follow as a by-product. Chase the by-product directly and you'll close nothing. The space sector is tiny; today's stranger is next year's hiring managercustomer, competitor, or co-investor.

Scripts — steal these

Reconnect a lapsed contact: "Hi [name] — it's been too long! I saw you're going to [event]. I'd genuinely love to catch up — free for a coffee between sessions? Either way, good to be in the same place again."
Pre-book a target (grad): "Hi [name], I'm finishing my [degree / programme] at [university] and following [specific thing their team does]. I'll be at [event] on [dates] — could I grab 15 minutes to hear how you got into [their area]? Not asking for a job, just learning. Happy to work around you."
Pre-book a target (founder): "Hi [name], I'm building [one line on what you do] and [specific reason they'd care — their portfolio / their problem]. I'll be at [event] [dates] — worth 20 minutes to compare notes on [their world]? Happy to come to you."
Optional depth — tiered objectives, meetings, go/no-go gut-check

Set tiered objectives (so you can tell if the trip worked)

  • P1 — Must (justifies the cost): e.g. 2 informational conversations + 1 referral ask.e.g. 3 qualified buyer convos + 1 investor follow-up secured.
  • P2 — Should: 5 new contacts in target orgs; reconnect 5 alumni.10 leads; 1 partnership conversation.
  • P3 — Nice: a press/social mention; one new idea worth chasing.

Pre-book the high-value meetings

Senior people barely touch the open floor — they run on pre-booked coffees, dinners, and the invite-only room. Book your priority 2–3 conversations before you arrive (20–30 min each). Use the event's matchmaking app if it has one.

30-second go/no-go gut-check (before you commit budget)

  • Can I name one specific outcome I'm going for? (If no → don't go.)
  • Will the people who can deliver it actually be there? (Verify, don't assume.)
  • Can I get into the real event — a meeting, a dinner, a warm intro? (That's where value lives.)
  • Is the true cost — ticket + travel + your days — worth that outcome?

Want the full weighted scorecard & portfolio view? That's in the Field Guide — but you don't need it to start. Crawl before you sprint.

2 · Floor — what to do in the room

Approach a stranger — the 5 steps

The question engine — your sharpest tool

Asking questions, especially follow-ups, is the cheapest way to be liked and remembered — it reads as listening. Talk less, ask more, chain the follow-up.

Evidence · Question-asking → liking, via perceived responsiveness — Huang et al., 2017, JPSP. More follow-up questions even won more second dates in a real speed-dating study.
The question bank — by what you're after
Professional everyone
  • "What are you actually working on right now — and what's the hard part?"
  • "What's changed most in your corner of the sector this year?"
  • "What would make this conference a win for you?"
Build the human everyone
  • "How did you end up in space? What was the path?"
  • "What's the part of the work you'd do even if no one paid you?"
Career & foot-in-the-door grad
  • "If you were starting over in this sector today, what would you do differently?"
  • "What does someone need to be good at to thrive on your team — beyond the obvious?"
  • "Who else should I be talking to?" — the highest-yield question at any conference. It compounds your network through theirs.
Customer validation founder
  • "How are you solving [problem] today? What does that cost you?"
  • "If something did [your value prop], would that change anything — or is it a vitamin, not a painkiller?"
  • "Who in your org would actually feel this pain? Who holds the budget?"
  • "What would have to be true for you to switch?"
Intelligence (open, never an interrogation) everyone
  • "What are you hearing about where the funding's going next year?"
  • "What's overhyped here? What's underrated?"
  • "What surprised you at this event?"
Protect your own info (counter-intel posture) everyone

Decide before the event what's public, shareable, and confidential. If a friendly stranger digs into your employer'sroadmap, pricing, or customers, deflect smoothly: "We're not talking about that publicly yet — but how are you thinking about it?" Discreet, not paranoid.

Graceful exits — leave without being rude

  • "I don't want to monopolise you — can I grab your details and follow up?"
  • "I promised myself I'd catch [session/person] — let's continue by email."
  • The generous exit: introduce them to someone first — "You two should meet, you're both on [X]" — then go. You leave and bank goodwill.

Working a booth founder

Attract → Understand → Explain → Decide. Never sit, never bury your face in your phone, never huddle with your team — it repels approach. Open with a question ("What brings you to the show?"), not a pitch. Qualify fast, then route or release.

Fast booth qualifying (30-second version)

You don't need the full framework on the floor — just triage. Quick additive score: Need +3 · Authority +2 · Timeline +2 · Engaged +1. 6+ = hot (book a meeting now), 3–5 = warm (capture + follow up), <3 = friendly card, move on. (This is the booth-speed version of BANT/MEDDIC.)

Working a booth as a job-seeker grad

Booths are staffed by people who actually do the work — gold for you. Skip "are you hiring?" Instead: "What does your team actually work on?" → "What's the hard part of that?" → "How did you get into it?" You're having an informational conversation, not begging. Get a name and a reason to follow up.

Capture every real contact — within 5 minutes (non-negotiable)

Your memory of who-was-who is gone by the next coffee break. Voice-note first if you must, then jot it. Minimum: name · org · the one next step · hot? A "send me something" = a hot signal → follow up within 24h.

Energy & the bar A 3-day conference is an endurance event. By day 2 afternoon, tired people make bad reads and forgettable impressions. Protect your energy like a budget — 8 good conversations beat 40 cards. The hotel bar builds relationships fast but has ended more careers than it's made: be warm, present, and remembered well — drink less than you want to.

3 · Follow-up — where the ROI is actually won

The cheapest edge in this entire playbook

~80% of conference leads get no follow-up at all. Doing it at all puts you ahead of four out of five people. Doing it within 48 hours — 24h for anyone hot — is when it actually converts. This is the step everyone skips and you won't.

Directional · Trade-show data (Momencio 2026; vendor reports): ~80% of leads never followed up; within-48h contact converts several times better than week-later. Treat numbers as directional — the direction is unambiguous: fast.
⏱ Do this the day you get back

Follow-up templates

Grad: "Hi [name] — great to meet you at [event]; I really enjoyed hearing about [specific thing]. [As promised: the link / the question I mentioned.] If you're open to it, I'd love to stay in touch as I move toward [their area]. Thanks again — and if [the person you suggested] is worth an intro, I'd be grateful."
Founder: "Hi [name] — great talking at [event] about [specific pain/topic]. As promised, [deck / one-pager / data]. Worth a 30-min call on [date] to go deeper on [their problem]? Either way, glad we connected."

10-minute debrief (the part that makes you better next time)

The experience without the debrief is just an expensive trip. Answer three things:

What worked / who mattered most?

What I'd do differently next time?

Verdict: worth coming back? (and did I hit my P1 goal?)

After (the edge)

Golden rules

Always end with a specific next step · quality > quantity · warm intros beat cold approaches · the small rooms (coffees, dinners) beat the big hall.
